At the edge of the Arctic, winter loosens its grip slowly. For eight months the tundra has lain frozen under a sky that barely saw the sun. Now meltwater runs in thin silver threads across the ground, and the first green shoots push up through the moss.

A herd of caribou moves north, thousands strong, following a route older than memory. Calves born only days ago must already keep pace. Overhead, snow geese arrive in loose white ribbons after a journey of three thousand miles. On a ridge, an Arctic fox still half in its winter coat listens for lemmings under the snow.

Summer here lasts only a few weeks. Everything that lives on the tundra must feed, breed, and raise its young before the light fades and the ice returns.
